My design principles

Conviction with curiosity

I ground my point of view in research, but I hold it loosely. Strong team decisions matter more than being right.

Progress over perfection

I'd rather ship and learn than wait for the perfect solution. Set direction, make calls, iterate.

Open to all ideas

Good ideas come from anywhere. I evaluate every suggestion by the same criteria: user impact, feasibility, and business goals.

How I use AI tools

I use AI throughout my process, from clarifying rough ideas to building tangible prototypes.

An example is a calorie tracking app I built, since existing ones on the market don't fit my needs. I started with half-baked ideas and used Claude to help me clarify my idea and write a PRD. From there I generated user flows and built wireframe prototypes. I then went through many rounds of iteration, asking Claude to evaluate my designs and think through the interactions with me. Once the wireframes felt solid, I moved into Figma to get to higher fidelity, then used Claude Code to build out the app itself. I don't know how to code, so having Claude Code handle that part means an idea can go from sketch to something real and testable, without me waiting on anyone else's time.

I bring this approach to my projects, using AI to think through ideas and build clickable prototypes quickly, so I can communicate my ideas clearly and move faster with my team.

How I approach designing AI tools

Building user trust is one of the hardest problems in designing AI products. I use a framework with two dimensions: how confident the AI is, and how reversible the decision is.

  • If the AI is confident and the decision is easy to reverse, let it act. At Intuit, QuickBooks automatically categorizes transactions, so an Amazon purchase becomes "office supplies." If it's wrong, the user corrects it and moves on. No harm done.
  • If the decision is hard to undo, or affects someone else, the user needs to stay in control. QuickBooks AI agents can draft an invoice or estimate, but a person has to hit send. Get that wrong and you can damage a customer relationship you don't get to undo.
  • And if the AI isn't confident, it should say so instead of presenting a guess as fact. It can still tell the user what it thinks, but the final decision is the user's.
